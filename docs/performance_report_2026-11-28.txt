BLOCKCRAFT REBUILD - PERFORMANCE: WHAT WATER AND CLOUDS COST (2026-11-28)

How it was measured
  tests2/bench.py, run twice on each of three builds, same seed (12345), same places, view distance 12, 1280 x 720:
    before-water  fe0c7f7 (light done, old water)
    water         60b7d4a (new water + faster loading)
    clouds        0a6c1d4 (clouds added)
  Plus a fair clouds on/off check: one settled scene, clouds switched on and off four times in a row.
  The test machine has NO graphics card: the browser draws with SwiftShader, a GPU emulated on 4 CPU cores. Frames take about
  half a second here, against a few milliseconds on a real card. So the numbers only compare builds with each other; they say
  nothing about the frame rate on an RTX 5070.

Results (median frame time in ms; two runs each)
                         before-water     water          clouds
  lake view              717 / 717        700 / 717      700 / 717
  sky view               500 / 517        500 / 500      533 / 550
  under water            517 / 500        517 / 517      550 / 567
  load a new world (s)   19.3 / 22.9      1.2 / 1.5      1.4 / 1.6
  mesh a section (ms)    1.23 / 1.47      1.49 / 1.31    1.69 / 1.37
  light a chunk (ms)     2.4 / 1.1        1.0 / 2.9      1.3 / 0.9
  JavaScript memory (MB) 321 / 325        359 / 331      342 / 338

  Clouds on/off, same scene, back to back (ms): 500 vs 533, 533 vs 533, 583 vs 600, 650 vs 650 (never slower with clouds on).

What it means
  - WATER: no measurable cost. The lake view, where water fills a third of the screen, runs the same in all three builds. The
    water shader (ripples, reflection, depth) only runs on water pixels and is small next to drawing the land. The mesher's new
    depth count may add a few percent per section, inside the noise (1.2-1.7 ms either way).
  - CLOUDS: no measurable cost. 4,596 triangles in one draw call, under 1% of the ~500,000-800,000 triangles of land in view.
    The cloud shape is rebuilt only when the player or the drift crosses a 12-block cell: 1.6 ms (once 9 ms with the garbage
    collector), at most a few times a second when flying fast.
    The 30-50 ms differences in the sky and underwater rows are noise: the same build varies that much between runs (frame
    times here move in steps of 16.7 ms), clouds are hidden under water anyway, and the on/off check shows no difference.
  - LOADING: about 15 times faster (20 s -> 1.5 s), from the water commit's change: the world is no longer drawn behind the
    loading screen, and meshing gets more time there.
  - Memory: no clear change (320-360 MB either way).

What does cost: the land itself
  Frame time follows the land's triangle count (480,000 -> 640,000 triangles: 500 -> 650 ms in the on/off check). At view
  distance 12 there are 500,000-800,000 triangles in 300-700 draw calls. A 3070-class card handles that easily, but at view
  distance 32 it would be roughly seven times as much. The biggest future gains are there, not in water or clouds:
    1. greedy meshing (merge flat runs of the same block face into one big quad): typically 3-5 times fewer triangles;
    2. fewer draw calls (one mesh per chunk rather than per 16-block section, or batching);
    3. distant land drawn as a cheap low-detail mesh (planned anyway: "distant land beyond the view distance").
